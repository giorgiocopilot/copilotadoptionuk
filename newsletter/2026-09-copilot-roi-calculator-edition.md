## 🎯 The $30 Question Nobody Answers Well

Every Copilot conversation ends in the same place: "What's the return?"

The cost side is easy — $30 per user per month. The value side is where leaders stall. Vague claims about "productivity gains" don't survive a finance review.

Here's what the data actually says.

**The enterprise case.** Forrester's Total Economic Impact™ study of Microsoft 365 Copilot — built from interviews with 16 decision-makers across 12 organizations and a survey of 367 respondents — found the composite enterprise gained **116% ROI over three years**, $19.7M in net present value, and payback in roughly 10 months. Each licensed user reclaimed about **9 hours per month**. [Forrester TEI, March 2025]

**The SMB case is even stronger.** Forrester's SMB study projects **132% to 353% ROI** over three years. Nearly a quarter of businesses cut time-to-market by 16–20%. [Forrester/Microsoft, October 2024]

**The catch.** Those returns assume people actually use it. Industry analyses show 30–40% of licenses are wasted within 90 days when rollouts skip training, use-case design, and executive sponsorship. ROI isn't bought with the license. It's built with adoption.

Sound familiar? Let's fix the math.

### Why Most Copilot Business Cases Fail

After reviewing dozens of enterprise proposals, I see the same three mistakes:

1. **Generic assumptions.** "Everyone saves an hour a day" gets laughed out of the boardroom. Finance wants your salaries, your headcount, your workflows.
2. **No breakeven line.** A three-year ROI projection means little without knowing the day cumulative savings pass cumulative cost.
3. **One scenario, no sensitivity.** If your ROI only works at 100% adoption, you don't have a business case. You have a hope.
